Funny enough, but I don’t think I believe in free will.

If we assume Newtonian physics holds, then everything in the universe should be determined by its previous state. Given the exact position and momentum of every particle, we could theoretically predict what happens next. This is basically Laplace’s demon AKA an imaginary intelligence that knows the state of every particle in the universe and can calculate the entire future.

Which is a rather depressing thought, because apparently my decision to procrastinate by writing this bs instead of studying (it’s w12 now btw) was already determined billions of years ago.

Well, to be precise, our brains are physical systems too right? Neurons fire because of electrochemical processes, which themselves obey the laws of physics. So if every thought I have is ultimately the result of physical interactions, then wouldn’t my decisions just be the inevitable consequences of everything that happened before them? I don’t really see at which point a bunch of atoms suddenly gains the ability to choose otherwise.

However, quantum mechanics makes this a little more complicated. At the quantum level, outcomes are described probabilistically, and depending on the interpretation, some events may be fundamentally random. But I don’t think randomness solves anything either. If my decisions are determined, then I couldn’t have chosen otherwise. If they’re random, then I didn’t really choose them in the first place.

Though I suppose this depends on what we mean by free will. Compatibilists argue that even in a deterministic universe, we’re still free as long as our actions come from our own desires rather than external coercion.

Which is fair enough, but aren’t those desires also determined by something?

I haven’t quite figured out whether that’s a genuine solution or just a more comforting definition of freedom.

Anyway, enough yapping about this. Whether I have free will or not I still need to finish my assignments and eventually join the rat race lol bye.
